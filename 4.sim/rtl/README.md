# Block-level benches

One directory per block. In it:

```
make                  # Verilator (default)
make SIM=icarus       # Icarus Verilog
make SIM=janushdl     # JanusHDL 0.21 or later
make wave             # GTKWave on output/wave.fst
```

The rules are in [`sim.mk`](sim.mk): `output/sim.log` and `output/wave.fst` with every simulator, timescale `1ns/1ns` unless a bench sets `TIMESCALE` (the TRNG bench: `1ns/1ps`), and `PLUSARGS` for the simulation (`make PLUSARGS=+trng_rings=1`).

## Self-checking DPE benches

`dpe`, `dpe_mux` and `dpe_demux` share [`dpe_tb_pkg.sv`](dpe_tb_pkg.sv), [`dpe_tb_src.sv`](dpe_tb_src.sv) and [`dpe_tb_sink.sv`](dpe_tb_sink.sv):

- A test packet (`test_pkt_t`) carries its destination, its length and its bytes. The bench keeps one per port in a struct with a field per port.
- `dpe_tb_src.send()` drives a packet: one byte per beat, `tkeep` `0x0001`, `tlast` on the last byte.
- `dpe_tb_sink` records what each output receives and checks it: every expected packet once, intact, with the right `tuser_src` and `tuser_dst`, nothing else, `tkeep` `0x0001` on every beat. Order is not checked.
- The bench prints `RESULT: PASS` or `RESULT: FAIL`. A FAIL fails `make`.

## System bench

`dpe_switch` runs the whole WireGuard data path with its one shared ChaCha20-Poly1305 core. The bench programs the routing and cryptokey tables over their CSR ports, then sends frames generated by [`sys_check.py`](dpe_switch/sys_check.py):

- plaintext to two peers comes out as WireGuard frames, and the send counters count up, also from one packet to the next back to back;
- WireGuard frames from the peers come out decrypted;
- a decrypted packet addressed to the other peer's network is encrypted again for that peer;
- the same with a 2032-byte packet (`AEAD_MAX_PT_BYTES`), twice, while `outp` stops for 4000 clocks: the shared core's decrypt side waits on its encrypt side, nothing lost;
- a spoofed inner source, a stale receiver index and a corrupted tag are dropped;
- a handshake goes to the CPU; transit WireGuard and WireGuard to this device on another port are routed unchanged; a WireGuard frame to us with EtherType ARP goes to the CPU.

`sys_check.py` checks every output frame byte for byte, together with its `tuser.dst` and `bypass_all`.
